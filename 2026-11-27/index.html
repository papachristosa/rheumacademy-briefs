Outside Prescriptions, the Lateral Hip and Zoledronate Fever

By Angelo Papachristos, PT, ACPAC

Several of this week's findings concern patients who are already inside a care pathway but are being missed by it, from high-risk prescriptions written outside rheumatology to vaccination gaps patients cannot see. The exercise evidence was stronger than usual: a Tai Chi trial outperformed an active home-exercise comparator in sarcopenic knee OA. A small randomised trial also gives infusion teams a concrete option for reducing fever after a first zoledronate infusion.

1.Most high-risk prescriptions in older rheumatic patients come from outside rheumatology

In older adults with rheumatic disease, 82.3% of high-risk medication prescriptions came from non-rheumatology clinicians. Among 45,455 US Medicare patients seen at rheumatology practices in the ACR's RISE registry (mean age 75.8, 76.9% female), 30.7% received at least one high-risk medication, and skeletal muscle relaxants and Z-drugs were among the common agents. Patients with axSpA (OR 1.40, 95% CI 1.20-1.62) and Sjögren's disease (OR 1.39, 95% CI 1.04-1.86) had the highest odds compared with RA.

AHP Takeaway: At any falls or function review in a patient over 65, run a full medication reconciliation that includes prescriptions from primary care and other specialists. Most of these agents were prescribed outside rheumatology, so ask about them directly rather than relying on the rheumatology medication list.

Source: Arthritis & Rheumatology · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43059569/

2.Three days of dexamethasone reduced fever after a first zoledronate infusion

In the control arm, 66.7% of first-time zoledronate recipients developed a fever of 37.8 °C or higher. In this open-label RCT of 50 analysed patients, oral dexamethasone 4 mg on the infusion day and the two days after reduced that to 26.9% (p = 0.01, NNT 2.5). The primary outcome, change in temperature from baseline over three days, favoured dexamethasone by 0.35 °C (95% CI 0.19 to 0.51). Rescue paracetamol use fell from 79.2% to 46.2% (p = 0.022). Sixty-two percent of participants had diabetes.

AHP Takeaway: For first-time zoledronate patients, raise dexamethasone prophylaxis with the prescribing team, who will weigh it against blood glucose in patients with diabetes. Update infusion-day education so it explains the acute-phase reaction and the option to reduce it. This is one small open-label trial, so any protocol change should go through the prescriber.

Source: Osteoporosis International · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43097007/

3.One in three hips awaiting replacement also has greater trochanteric pain syndrome

Picture a woman in her late 60s on the hip replacement list who points to the side of her thigh rather than her groin. In a cross-sectional study of 340 hips with advanced OA awaiting THA, clinical GTPS was confirmed by a test cluster in 107 of 332 evaluable hips (32.2%), with a 3:1 female predominance. After adjustment for age, sex and BMI, the dual-pathology group had worse WOMAC function (MD 4.76 points, 95% CI 0.90-8.61).

AHP Takeaway: When a hip OA patient reports lateral hip pain, run a GTPS test cluster before attributing every symptom to the joint. A positive cluster identifies a second problem you can start treating, for example with gluteal tendon loading, while the patient waits for surgery.

Source: Osteoarthritis and Cartilage · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43085718/
